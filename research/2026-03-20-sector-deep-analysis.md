## Sector 1: US Big Tech (AI Software Layer)

### Industry Analysis

The Big Tech cohort has become the defining AI monetization layer of the 2020s. These companies own the distribution (billions of users), the compute infrastructure (hyperscale cloud), and increasingly the models (Azure OpenAI, Gemini, Llama). Competitive dynamics are unusual: the giants both compete and co-depend on each other. Microsoft needs NVDA chips; NVDA needs Microsoft's Azure workloads; Meta needs TSMC for its AI silicon.

The structural tailwind is AI monetization converting from CapEx spending phase → revenue recognition phase in 2026. Azure, Copilot, and advertising AI enhancements are translating to measurable revenue growth. The risk is margin compression from the capex arms race — Meta alone plans to spend $115–135B on capex in 2026, nearly double 2025's $72B.

## Sector 2: Memory / RAM

### Industry Analysis

Memory is the most cyclical segment in tech — DRAM and NAND prices collapsed 50–70% in 2022–2023 as the PC/smartphone cycle corrected. Now in a sharp upcycle driven by three structural factors:

1. **HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)** — every NVIDIA H100/B200 GPU requires HBM3/HBM4 in significant quantities. Only SK Hynix, Micron, and Samsung can produce it. This is a structural demand shift, not a cyclical bump.
2. **DDR5 server transition** — cloud providers upgrading DRAM for AI servers, driving ASP uplift over DDR4.
3. **Supply discipline** — major DRAM makers have constrained commodity capacity, redirecting to high-margin HBM/DDR5.

**The bear thesis:** China's CXMT (Chang Xin Memory) is scaling commodity DRAM with state subsidies, threatening to compress the cycle as it did in solar/EVs. The Nanya 2408.TW commodity exposure is most vulnerable.

**Key signal to watch:** DRAM spot prices on DRAMeXchange. When they peak and roll over, exit before guidance cuts.

## Sector 3: Taiwan Semiconductor Supply Chain

### Industry Analysis

Taiwan is the irreplaceable node in the global AI chip supply chain. TSMC's 3nm/2nm monopoly cannot be replicated (Intel 18A is 2–3 years behind; Samsung has yield issues). Beyond TSMC, the AI server supply chain flows through: ABF substrates → advanced packaging (CoWoS, SoIC) → ODM server assembly (Wiwynn, Quanta) → data center deployment.

**The hidden gems are the ODMs.** Wiwynn and Quanta build the physical AI servers that hyperscalers rack in their data centers. They're growing 150%+ but trade at 9–11x fwd P/E because they're TW-listed, thin-margin, and underfollowed outside Taiwan.

**Taiwan Strait geopolitical risk** is the permanent discount — real but manageable with appropriate position sizing. Diversified across multiple TW names reduces single-event risk.
