## 1. 產業分析 (Industry position & moat)

**Solar conductive paste — duopoly-plus market:**
- Top 3 (Heraeus 22%, DuPont 18%, Samsung SDI) ≈ 68% global share. Giga Solar is in the tier-2 group alongside DK, Changzhou Fusion, Soltrium, Monocrystal.
- China makes ~92% of front-side paste *production volume*, but the high-margin nodes (TOPCon/HJT/BC) are still dominated by Heraeus/DuPont/Giga Solar/Samsung. This is the key competitive cushion: Giga Solar is NOT competing head-on with Soltrium on commodity PERC paste — they're on the higher-spec n-type front.
- Industry secular: PV silver paste TAM $4.6B (2024) → $10.96B (2032), CAGR 7.1%. N-type cells (TOPCon 1.5x, HJT 2x silver/W vs PERC) drive paste *value* growth even as silver-thrifting trims 2–3%/yr.
- Real risk: silver-coated-copper hybrid pastes (HJT-targeted, being commercialized 2025–2027) could leapfrog incumbents if Giga Solar isn't on the formulation roadmap. Management mentions TOPCon and BC paste in earnings calls — not HJT-Cu hybrids. **Watchpoint.**

**Diversification — credible or scattered?**
- **US module manufacturing (in-house, not paste):** Q4'25 began contributing revenue. This is what's driving the massive YoY revenue growth — it's a tariff arbitrage play on the post-2024 US polysilicon/module tariff structure. Real, but it's a *capacity-build* story with thin margins, not a moat.
- **AI 散熱 (碩通 / 華旭):** Cooling material supply chain entry. CAGR 25–35%. But the dominant TW cooling names (奇鋐 3017, 雙鴻 3324) own AWS/Meta/NVIDIA pipelines — Giga Solar is a tier-3 entrant with **no specific named OEM design wins** in any source I could find. This is a theme buy, not an order book.
- **LFP / SiO 負極 / nano-Si battery materials:** Still loss-making in 2024 (NT$210M loss). Taiwan production line "starts 2025." No named EV/storage OEM customer disclosed. Foxconn EV platform mentioned in one moneyweekly piece via subsidiary 鑫鴻能源 — needs verification.
- **SiC semiconductor materials (華旭):** Mentioned but no scale.

**Assessment:** The paste business has a real but eroding moat. The diversification narrative is the right strategic move but execution evidence is thin — they have *participation* in AI cooling and LFP, not *position*. The US module factory is the only diversification that's *already* showing up in the P&L.

**Key debate:** Is the US module factory a permanent margin engine or a 2026 spike that fades when subsidies/tariffs reset? Bears think it's a tariff-window arbitrage with a sunset; bulls think it's a 2026–2028 multi-year ramp.
