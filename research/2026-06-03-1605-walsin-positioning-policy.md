# 華新 Walsin Lihwa (1605.TW) — Positioning & Policy-Driven Fundamental Analysis

*Date: 2026-06-03 | Price ~NT$32–34.5 | Quant scan: HOLD, 17% confidence | Not financial advice*

This note deliberately looks past the financial statements. Walsin's story is a **policy-and-positioning** thesis: it is re-rating from a cyclical commodity processor (copper / stainless / nickel) into a **Taiwan energy-infrastructure supplier** riding three multi-decade policy waves. What makes 2026 interesting is that **all three legs can inflect at once** — and each is backed by a concrete government program, not just a narrative.

---

## Business Mix (the starting point)

| Segment | Role | 2026 state |
|---|---|---|
| **Wire & Cable (銅)** | Taiwan grid + offshore-wind subsea cable | Growth engine & bull case |
| **Stainless / Specialty Steel** | Commodity-cyclical, incl. EU specialty steel | Slow recovery |
| **Resources (鎳, Indonesia)** | Upstream nickel pig iron / nickel matte | Loss-making — the drag (but turning?) |

Recent prints: 2025 revenue NT$174.2bn (−2.83% YoY); 2026 Q1 EPS NT$0.81 (+376% YoY, +252% QoQ). Sell-side (thin, ~1 analyst) target NT$36.6, FY25 EPS ~0.73 → FY26 EPS ~1.39.

---

## Leg 1 — Taiwan Grid: 強韌電網 (highest conviction)

Policy-funded, not speculative — the anchor of the thesis.

- **NT$564.5bn / 10-year** Taipower grid-resilience plan. **NT$437.9bn** goes to **distributed-grid construction (分散電網)** — the underground/HV cable + substation work Walsin supplies. NT$125bn on grid hardening, NT$1.69bn on system defense.
- **28 new substations + 24 indoor-conversion substations**; 3 hub-node dispersal projects.
- Spend profile: **~35% before 2026, ~65% across 2026–2032** → the order book *accelerates* from here. Revenue visibility to ~2032.
- Walsin's NT$19bn+ 161kV XLPE cable tender win is the first tranche of a decade of this.

**Why durable:** the driver is blackout prevention (post-303/513 outages) + surging **AI/datacenter power demand** + net-zero — politically very hard to walk back. This is not a cuttable subsidy.

## Leg 2 — Offshore Wind: subsea cable localization (high reward, moat-erosion risk)

Walsin's Kaohsiung subsea-cable JV with NKT (WECS) is Taiwan's **only** domestic subsea power-cable plant, and subsea cable is a designated **國產化 (localization)** item.

- **3-1 (2.64GW, grid 2026–27)** and **3-2 (2.70GW)** still carry localization requirements → captive domestic demand.
- **3-3 (~3.6GW, bids 2026, grid 2030–31) drops mandatory localization** and zero-dollar bidding. The EU has also challenged Taiwan's localization rules at the **WTO**.
- WECS timeline: completed end-2025, **2026 certification/trial production, revenue 2027+**.

**Read:** strong 2026–2029 story for the protected 3-1/3-2 build-out, but the **policy moat weakens at 3-3**. WECS must convert into genuine cost-competitiveness before localization protection fades. Watch for *export / non-protected* orders, not just captive domestic ones.

## Leg 3 — Indonesia Nickel: the drag that could turn (2026 policy tailwind)

The loss-making resources segment is the part most likely to surprise upward on policy.

- Indonesia is **cutting the 2026 RKAB mining quota ~34%** (379mt → 260–270mt), deliberately to **support nickel prices** after years of self-inflicted oversupply. Supply gap could exceed 100mt.
- Product priority: **MHP > nickel matte (冰鎳) > ferronickel**. Walsin's Sunny Metal (青山 JV, 50.1% stake, US$380m / >NT$10bn total) is a **nickel-matte** plant — in the favored middle tier, above the contracting ferronickel capacity.

**Read:** the cash-bleeding upstream bet sits in front of a policy-engineered supply tightening. If Indonesian nickel prices recover in 2026, the segment can swing from drag to contributor. **Caveat:** RKAB policy has flip-flopped repeatedly — the least reliable of the three legs.

## Macro Backdrop — the Copper Super-Cycle

All three legs ride a structural copper-demand wave:

- S&P Global / UBS: **copper deficit ~320–400k t in 2026**, inventories ~14 days of demand; demand 28mt (2025) → 42mt by 2040 on AI datacenters + EVs + grid.
- A single hyperscale AI datacenter can need **~50,000t of copper**; global grid upgrades are a **$7.5tn** investment wave.

For Walsin this is double-edged but **net-structurally positive**: high copper gives short-term inventory gains *and* a cost-pass-through challenge, but the **durable win is cable volume** — grid + datacenter + offshore wind all consume Walsin's products. Volume matters more than the copper-price trade.

## Synthesis — What You're Buying

| Leg | Policy driver | Conviction | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid cable | 強韌電網 NT$564.5bn / 10yr | **Highest** — funded, backloaded to 2026-32 | Now → 2032 |
| Subsea cable | Offshore wind 3-1/3-2 localization | High near-term, **moat fades at 3-3** | 2027+ revenue |
| Nickel | Indonesia RKAB cut → price support | Medium — policy is fickle | 2026 inflection |

**Bull (structural):** a leveraged domestic-champion play on Taiwan's two largest energy-capex programs of the decade, with an upstream nickel option about to get a policy tailwind, all on a structural copper deficit. The consensus earnings doubling (FY25 ~0.73 → FY26 ~1.39) is the *first year* of this, not a peak.

**Bear (structural):** still a conglomerate where two of three legs are commodity-cyclical and policy-dependent. The subsea moat erodes once 3-3 drops localization; Indonesian nickel policy can reverse; grid capex is backloaded and execution-dependent; and infrastructure re-rating stories get priced in fast (already run to multi-year highs).

**Monitor (non-financial signals):**
1. **Pace of Taipower tender awards** — the real order-book proxy; the leg that justifies a re-rating.
2. **WECS certification → first commercial subsea contract** (especially any non-protected/export order).
3. **Indonesian nickel price + whether the RKAB cut actually holds.**
4. **Copper-stripped gross margin** — to separate durable cable economics from inventory-gain noise.

**Bottom line:** the grid leg alone justifies owning a piece of the story for the decade; the subsea and nickel legs are the upside optionality that makes 2026 the inflection year — but they're also where the policy risk lives. A "right direction, watch the execution and the entry price" name rather than a clean buy.
